Fund Flow

How money moves from payer to payee, from collection to payout, reconciliation, and settlement.

COBO + POBO

COBO · Collection On Behalf OfA platform collects money for a merchant or subsidiary. Funds enter an account operated by the platform while the economic owner is another party.
POBO · Payment On Behalf OfA platform pays suppliers, sellers, or partners on behalf of another company.
Omnibus AccountA pooled account where funds for many users are held together, with internal ledgers tracking each user's balance.
ReconciliationMatching invoices, payments, fees, refunds, and settlement records so finance knows which money belongs to which transaction.

Crypto analogy: an exchange omnibus wallet often feels like a user balance, but the chain sees pooled custody and internal ledgering.

Card Acquiring

The card-payment stack from checkout to authorization, capture, clearing, settlement, disputes, and merchant payout.

Core roles

CardholderThe buyer who owns and uses the payment card.
IssuerThe bank or fintech that issued the card and approves or declines the authorization.
Card SchemeVisa, Mastercard, Amex, UnionPay, and other networks that set rules and route messages.
AcquirerThe merchant's financial institution for card acceptance and settlement.
GatewayThe technical layer that encrypts and routes payment requests.
ProcessorThe infrastructure provider that handles authorization, clearing, and settlement files.

Trade Payments

B2B collection, invoices, payment terms, supplier payouts, and working-capital instruments.

Invoice-led payments

InvoiceA document stating who pays whom, for what, how much, and by when.
BeneficiaryThe party that ultimately receives the money.
RemitterThe party sending the payment.
Net 30A payment term requiring payment within 30 days of invoice receipt.
AP / ARAccounts payable and accounts receivable. The two finance workflows around bills and collections.
EscrowA neutral holding arrangement where funds are released only after conditions are met.

FX & Cross-Border

Why international money movement takes time, loses value, and needs banks, messages, and liquidity corridors.

Cross-border primitives

SWIFTA global messaging network for bank instructions. It moves messages, not the money itself.
Correspondent BankA bank that holds accounts for another bank to support international payments.
Nostro / VostroMirror terms for accounts one bank holds with another in a foreign currency.
Mid-market RateThe midpoint between buy and sell prices in the wholesale FX market.
FX SpreadThe difference between the mid-market rate and the rate quoted to the user.
Cut-off TimeThe daily deadline after which a payment is processed on the next business day.

Crypto Payments

Stablecoin payments, wallets, on-chain settlement, ramps, and crypto-native checkout flows.

Stablecoin payment terms

StablecoinA token designed to maintain a stable value, commonly pegged to USD.
USDT / USDCThe two most common USD stablecoins used for payments and settlement.
On-rampThe path from fiat money into crypto or stablecoins.
Off-rampThe path from crypto or stablecoins back into fiat bank money.
Settlement FinalityThe point after which a transaction is practically irreversible.
Gas FeeThe network fee paid to execute a blockchain transaction.

Crypto Cards

How cards funded by crypto balances connect wallets, issuers, card networks, and merchant acceptance.

Crypto card stack

Crypto CardA card product where spending is funded by a crypto or stablecoin balance, often converted before card settlement.
Program ManagerThe company that packages card operations, user experience, compliance, and partner orchestration.
BIN SponsorA licensed institution that sponsors the card program into a card network.
Spend AuthorizationThe decision to approve or decline a card spend based on balance, compliance, and risk checks.

Compliance

Identity, business verification, transaction monitoring, sanctions screening, and regulatory reporting.

Core compliance terms

KYCKnow Your Customer. Verifying the identity of an individual customer.
KYBKnow Your Business. Verifying a company, its ownership, and its controllers.
AMLAnti-Money Laundering. Controls to detect and prevent criminal financial activity.
Sanctions ScreeningChecking users and counterparties against restricted-party lists.
Travel RuleA requirement that originator and beneficiary information travels with qualifying transfers.
VASPVirtual Asset Service Provider, such as an exchange, custodian, broker, or transfer platform.

License Map

Payments are territorial. There is no single license that lets a company collect and pay everywhere.

Regional licenses

US MTLMoney Transmitter License, obtained state by state in the United States.
EU EMI / PIElectronic Money Institution and Payment Institution licenses under European frameworks.
UK FCA E-moneyUK authorization for e-money issuance and payment services.
Singapore MPIMajor Payment Institution license regulated by MAS.
Hong Kong MSO / SVFMoney service and stored-value facility licensing categories.
Dubai VARAVirtual asset licensing framework for Dubai.

Stablecoin Licenses

Stablecoin issuance and service activity may trigger e-money, payment, custody, broker, or virtual-asset rules.

Stablecoin regulatory map

IssuerThe entity that mints and redeems the stablecoin and manages reserve obligations.
ReserveThe cash, bank deposits, T-bills, or other assets backing the stablecoin.
MiCA EMTEU e-money token category under the Markets in Crypto-Assets framework.
CASPCrypto-Asset Service Provider under EU MiCA.
Custody LicenseAuthorization to hold crypto assets or private keys for clients.
Redemption RightThe user's legal or contractual right to exchange tokens back for fiat value.

Institutional Roles

Payment companies are networks of licensed institutions, banks, processors, compliance vendors, and liquidity partners.

Who does what

PSPPayment Service Provider. A company that helps merchants accept and manage payments.
PayFacPayment Facilitator. A platform that onboards sub-merchants under a master merchant structure.
Banking PartnerA bank that provides accounts, payment rails, sponsorship, or safeguarding.
Liquidity ProviderA partner supplying FX, crypto, or fiat liquidity for conversion and settlement.
Compliance VendorA provider for KYC, KYB, AML monitoring, sanctions, device risk, or fraud signals.
CustodianA licensed entity that holds client assets or private keys.

Transaction Lifecycle

The stages a transaction passes through before it becomes final, posted, reconciled, and paid out.

Lifecycle checkpoints

AuthorizationThe issuer approves a payment request and places a hold on funds.
CaptureThe merchant confirms the transaction should be charged.
ClearingTransaction data and fees are exchanged between institutions.
SettlementNet funds move between banks or counterparties.
RefundThe merchant sends money back after a transaction has settled.
ChargebackA cardholder dispute routed through the issuer and card network.

Business Metrics

The operating metrics investors and payment teams use to understand scale, monetization, retention, and risk.

Metrics quick reference

TPVTotal Payment Volume. The value of payments processed through the platform.
GMVGross Merchandise Volume. Total commerce value, including transactions not processed by the platform's own payment stack.
Take RateNet revenue divided by payment volume.
NRRNet Revenue Retention, measuring expansion and contraction across existing customers.
LTV / CACLifetime value divided by customer acquisition cost.
ARR / MRRAnnual or monthly recurring revenue from subscriptions or platform fees.
