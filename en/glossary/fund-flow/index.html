Fund Flow

How money moves from payer to payee. This section mirrors the Chinese fund-flow chapter: collection, payout, clearing, settlement, netting, reconciliation, ledgers, pooled accounts, reserves, and treasury controls.

COBO + POBO — collection and payout on behalf of another party

COBO · Collection On Behalf OfA platform collects money for a merchant, subsidiary, seller, or user. The payer sees the platform or a collection account, while the underlying economic owner is another party.
POBO · Payment On Behalf OfA platform pays suppliers, sellers, creators, drivers, contractors, or group entities on behalf of another company.
Omnibus AccountA pooled bank or wallet account holding many users' funds together, while internal ledgers track each user's balance and movements.
Ledger BalanceThe balance shown in the platform's internal books. It may differ from bank balance because of pending transactions, fees, reversals, and reserves.

Example: a marketplace collects customer funds into one bank account, records each seller's receivable internally, then pays sellers out later. In crypto, an exchange omnibus wallet follows the same logic: one chain address, many internal user balances.

Clearing vs Settlement vs Netting

ClearingCalculating who owes what after authorization or transaction submission. It includes transaction data, fees, interchange, scheme fees, FX, and dispute adjustments.
SettlementThe actual movement of net funds between banks, schemes, processors, merchants, or wallets.
NettingOffsetting payables and receivables so only the net amount is moved. This reduces bank transfers and liquidity needs.
Gross SettlementEvery transaction settles individually without offsetting. It is clearer, but more expensive and liquidity-heavy.

A card network may clear millions of transactions, net each participant's position, and settle only the final net amount. Stablecoin transfers often look like gross settlement because each on-chain transfer is visible individually.

Balances, reserves, and reconciliation

Available BalanceFunds the merchant can actually withdraw or use after pending transactions, reserves, and holds are excluded.
Pending BalanceFunds expected to settle but not yet available because clearing, risk review, or banking cut-offs are not complete.
Rolling ReserveA percentage of merchant volume held back for a period to cover refunds, disputes, fraud, or future chargebacks.
Chargeback ReserveA specific reserve against expected card disputes and chargeback liabilities.
ReconciliationMatching transactions, invoices, bank statements, processor reports, refunds, fees, and settlements until every cent has an owner.
Settlement ReportA processor or bank file showing transactions, fees, adjustments, and the final payout amount.

Fund-flow operating controls

SafeguardingKeeping customer funds separate from company operating funds, commonly required for e-money and payment institutions.
FloatMoney temporarily held by a platform between collection and payout. It creates liquidity and compliance obligations.
TreasuryThe function that manages cash, liquidity, bank accounts, FX exposure, reserves, and settlement timing.
Four-eye ApprovalA control requiring two independent approvals before large payouts, account changes, or treasury movements.

Card Acquiring

The card-payment stack from checkout to authorization, capture, clearing, settlement, disputes, merchant payout, fees, authentication, and PCI scope.

The five visible roles in a card payment

CardholderThe buyer who owns and uses the card.
MerchantThe business selling goods or services and accepting the card payment.
IssuerThe bank or fintech that issued the card, holds the cardholder relationship, and approves or declines authorization.
Card Scheme / NetworkVisa, Mastercard, Amex, UnionPay, and similar networks that set rules, route messages, and calculate fees.
AcquirerThe merchant-side financial institution that enables card acceptance and ultimately receives settlement for the merchant.
ProcessorThe technical operator handling authorization messages, clearing files, settlement files, and reporting.

Gateway, PSP, PayFac, ISO

Payment GatewayThe technical entry point that tokenizes, encrypts, and routes payment data from checkout to processor or acquirer.
PSP · Payment Service ProviderA merchant-facing platform that combines gateway, acquiring connectivity, payment methods, reporting, and operations.
PayFac · Payment FacilitatorA platform that onboards sub-merchants under a master merchant account, reducing onboarding friction but taking more risk.
ISO · Independent Sales OrganizationA sales or distribution partner that refers merchants to an acquirer or processor.

Stripe and Adyen are often discussed as PSPs, but their exact role varies by region, merchant setup, and license stack.

Transaction lifecycle inside cards

Authorization / AuthThe issuer checks card validity, funds, risk, and permissions, then places a hold. No final money movement yet.
CaptureThe merchant confirms that the authorized payment should be charged.
ClearingTransaction details and fees are exchanged between acquirer, network, and issuer.
SettlementNet funds move through the card network and banking rails.
PayoutThe PSP or acquirer transfers available funds to the merchant bank account.
VoidCanceling an authorization before capture. The hold is released, and the transaction never becomes a charge.

Card fees and risk terms

MDR · Merchant Discount RateThe total card acceptance cost paid by the merchant, usually shown as a percentage plus a fixed fee.
Interchange FeeThe fee paid to the issuer. It is often the largest component of card cost.
Scheme FeeFees charged by card networks for routing, assessment, cross-border processing, and other network services.
Acquirer MarkupThe acquirer or PSP's commercial margin on top of interchange and scheme fees.
3DS / SCAAuthentication flows that ask the cardholder to prove identity, often shifting fraud liability.
PCI DSSSecurity standards for handling cardholder data. Hosted checkout can reduce a merchant's PCI scope.

Disputes, chargebacks, and reversals

RefundThe merchant voluntarily sends funds back after settlement.
ChargebackThe cardholder disputes through the issuer, and funds may be pulled back from the merchant.
RepresentmentThe merchant's response package arguing that the chargeback is invalid.
ARN · Acquirer Reference NumberA tracking identifier used to trace refunds and card settlements through the acquiring chain.

Trade Payments

B2B collection, invoices, payment terms, escrow, supplier payouts, letters of credit, trade finance, and the operational gap between delivery and payment.

Invoice-led payments

InvoiceA document stating who pays whom, for what, how much, in what currency, and by when.
InvoicingThe end-to-end process of issuing, sending, collecting, matching, and closing an invoice.
BeneficiaryThe party that ultimately receives the money.
RemitterThe party sending the payment.
Payment TermsThe agreed timing and conditions for payment, such as Net 30, due on receipt, or milestone-based payment.
Net 30 / Net 60Payment must be made within 30 or 60 days after invoice receipt or issue date.

B2B finance operations

AP · Accounts PayableThe buyer-side workflow for bills owed to suppliers.
AR · Accounts ReceivableThe seller-side workflow for invoices waiting to be collected.
Purchase Order / POA buyer-issued document confirming intent to purchase under agreed terms.
Goods ReceiptA record that goods or services were received, often needed before AP approves payment.
Three-way MatchMatching purchase order, invoice, and goods receipt before paying a supplier.
Remittance AdviceA notice explaining what invoice or invoices a payment is intended to settle.

Escrow and trade assurance

EscrowA neutral holding arrangement where funds are released only after agreed conditions are met.
Milestone PaymentPayment released in stages as project milestones are completed.
Letter of Credit / LCA bank guarantee used in international trade, promising payment if documents meet the agreed conditions.
Documentary CollectionA trade process where banks handle shipping and payment documents but do not guarantee payment.

Trade finance and supplier liquidity

FactoringSelling receivables to a finance provider for earlier cash.
Invoice DiscountingBorrowing against receivables while keeping customer collection in-house.
Dynamic DiscountingThe buyer pays earlier in exchange for a discount.
Supply Chain FinanceA program where suppliers get early payment based on the buyer's credit quality.

FX & Cross-Border

Cross-border money movement is a stack of messaging, bank accounts, FX conversion, compliance checks, cut-off times, local rails, and liquidity partners.

SWIFT and correspondent banking

SWIFTA secure bank messaging network. It transmits instructions, not the money itself.
Correspondent BankA bank that holds accounts for another bank to support international payments.
Intermediary BankA bank in the middle of a transfer chain. It may deduct fees and add delay.
Nostro AccountOur account with your bank, typically in a foreign currency.
Vostro AccountYour account with our bank, from the receiving bank's perspective.
MT103A SWIFT customer credit transfer message often used to trace international wires.

FX pricing

Mid-market RateThe midpoint between buy and sell prices in the wholesale FX market.
SpreadThe difference between the mid-market rate and the rate quoted to the customer.
MarkupThe provider's commercial margin added into the FX quote.
SlippageThe difference between expected and executed rate, often caused by volatility or low liquidity.
Spot FXA currency exchange for near-immediate settlement, commonly T+0 to T+2.
Forward FXA contract locking in an exchange rate for a future date.

Settlement timing and rails

Cut-off TimeThe daily deadline after which payment processing moves to the next business day.
Value DateThe date funds are considered settled and available.
ACHA US batch bank transfer rail, often cheaper but slower than instant payment rails.
SEPAEuro-area bank transfer schemes, including SEPA Credit Transfer and SEPA Instant.
FPS / Faster PaymentsLocal fast bank-transfer systems such as UK Faster Payments or Hong Kong FPS.
RTGSReal-time gross settlement systems used for high-value bank payments.

Why cross-border card payments lose value

Cross-border FeeA card network or acquirer fee applied when issuer, merchant, or settlement countries differ.
DCC · Dynamic Currency ConversionOffering the cardholder a local-currency price at checkout, often with an embedded FX markup.
Withholding / ReserveFunds held back for risk, refunds, and disputes, especially for cross-border merchants.
Beneficiary DeductionFees deducted along the transfer path before the final beneficiary receives funds.

Crypto Payments

Stablecoin payments, wallets, on-chain settlement, ramps, custody, token standards, chain selection, and crypto-native checkout flows.

Stablecoin primitives

StablecoinA token designed to maintain stable value, most often pegged to USD.
USDTTether's USD-pegged stablecoin, widely used for global crypto payments.
USDCCircle's USD-pegged stablecoin, often used in regulated and institutional flows.
ReserveThe cash, bank deposits, T-bills, or other assets backing a stablecoin.
MintIssuing new tokens after reserve assets are received.
Burn / RedeemDestroying tokens and returning fiat or reserve value to the holder.

Checkout, invoices, and payment links

Crypto CheckoutA checkout flow where the buyer pays with wallet funds or stablecoins rather than card rails.
Payment LinkA URL containing amount, asset, network, merchant, and payment instructions.
QR PaymentA wallet-scannable representation of the payment request.
On-chain ReceiptThe transaction hash and chain data proving payment occurred.
WebhookA server callback notifying the merchant system that a payment was detected or confirmed.
Confirmation CountThe number of blocks after a transaction, used to judge settlement confidence.

Wallet and network vocabulary

Wallet AddressA public blockchain address used to receive assets.
Private KeyThe secret that controls asset movement. Whoever controls it controls the funds.
Seed PhraseA human-readable backup for wallet keys.
Gas FeeThe network fee paid to execute a blockchain transaction.
Token StandardRules such as ERC-20, TRC-20, or SPL defining how tokens behave on a chain.
BridgeInfrastructure for moving value or representations of value across chains.

Ramps and liquidity

On-rampFiat into crypto or stablecoins.
Off-rampCrypto or stablecoins back into fiat bank money.
CEXCentralized exchange. Easier onboarding and liquidity, but assets are typically custodial.
DEXDecentralized exchange. On-chain swaps without a central order custodian.
OTC DeskOver-the-counter trading desk for larger conversions and negotiated pricing.
Settlement FinalityThe point after which the transfer is practically irreversible.

Crypto Cards

Crypto cards connect wallet balances, stablecoins, issuer processors, BIN sponsors, card networks, compliance, authorization, and merchant acceptance.

Crypto card operating model

Crypto CardA card product where spending is funded by crypto or stablecoin balances, often converted before card settlement.
Prepaid CardA card funded in advance from a stored balance rather than a credit line.
Debit CardA card that draws from a deposit or wallet balance.
Virtual CardA card number issued digitally for online spending.
Physical CardA plastic or metal card used at POS terminals and ATMs.
Card FundingThe process that moves wallet value into the card spend account or available balance.

Program stack

BIN SponsorA licensed financial institution sponsoring the card program into a card network.
Issuer ProcessorThe processor that authorizes card spend, manages balances, and connects to networks.
Program ManagerThe company orchestrating card UX, compliance, operations, vendors, and commercial rules.
Card NetworkVisa, Mastercard, or another network handling acceptance and rules.
Settlement AccountThe fiat account used to settle card network obligations.
Float AccountA funding account holding value available for card authorization.

Authorization and conversion

Spend AuthorizationReal-time decision to approve or decline a card transaction based on balance, risk, compliance, and limits.
Pre-fundingHolding enough fiat or stablecoin liquidity in advance to support card settlement.
Just-in-time FundingMoving or converting funds at the moment of authorization.
Crypto-to-Fiat ConversionConverting stablecoins or crypto to fiat so the card network can settle in supported currencies.
FX MarkupAdditional spread or fee when the card spend currency differs from the funding currency.
Velocity LimitA risk rule limiting spend frequency or amount over a time window.

Compliance

Identity, business verification, sanctions, AML monitoring, fraud signals, Travel Rule, risk scoring, reporting, and evidence trails.

KYC, KYB, AML, CFT

KYC · Know Your CustomerVerifying the identity of an individual customer.
KYB · Know Your BusinessVerifying a company, its registration, business activity, ownership, and controllers.
UBO · Ultimate Beneficial OwnerThe natural person who ultimately owns or controls a business.
AML · Anti-Money LaunderingControls for detecting and preventing money laundering.
CFT / CTFCountering the financing of terrorism.
EDD · Enhanced Due DiligenceDeeper review for higher-risk users, geographies, industries, or transaction patterns.

Screening and monitoring

Sanctions ScreeningChecking customers and counterparties against restricted-party lists such as OFAC, UN, EU, or UK lists.
PEP ScreeningIdentifying politically exposed persons whose positions create higher corruption or bribery risk.
Adverse MediaNegative news or public records used in risk assessment.
Transaction MonitoringRules and models that flag unusual or suspicious behavior.
Risk ScoreA numeric or categorical rating combining identity, geography, behavior, device, and transaction signals.
Case ManagementThe workflow for reviewing alerts, documenting decisions, and escalating suspicious activity.

Crypto compliance

VASPVirtual Asset Service Provider, such as an exchange, custodian, broker, transfer platform, or hosted wallet provider.
Travel RuleA requirement that originator and beneficiary information travels with qualifying transfers.
Wallet ScreeningChecking blockchain addresses for sanctions, hacks, scams, mixers, dark markets, or high-risk exposure.
Source of FundsEvidence explaining where the money came from.
Source of WealthEvidence explaining how the customer accumulated wealth.
Suspicious Activity ReportA regulatory report filed when activity may indicate money laundering, fraud, or other illicit finance.

License Map

Payments are territorial. A company operating globally usually needs a stack of local licenses, registrations, exemptions, bank partners, and compliance programs.

United States and Canada

MSB RegistrationUS federal money services business registration with FinCEN. It is not the same as state licensing.
MTL · Money Transmitter LicenseState-by-state US licensing for money transmission.
BitLicenseNew York's virtual currency business license, one of the strictest US crypto regimes.
National Bank CharterA federal bank charter that can change state licensing requirements but has a much higher entry bar.
FINTRAC MSBCanadian money services business registration and AML supervision.
State Money Movement ExemptionsAgency, payment processor, closed-loop, or other exemptions that may apply only under narrow facts.

Europe and the UK

EMI · Electronic Money InstitutionAuthorization to issue e-money and provide payment services.
PI · Payment InstitutionAuthorization for payment services without issuing e-money.
Small EMI / Small PILower-scale regimes with limits on volume, geography, or services.
PassportingEU mechanism allowing a licensed institution in one member state to operate across other member states.
FCA E-moneyUK e-money and payment authorization after Brexit, separate from EU passporting.
AISP / PISPPSD2 account information and payment initiation service provider permissions.

Asia-Pacific and Middle East

Singapore MPIMajor Payment Institution license regulated by MAS, covering payment services above specified thresholds.
DPT ServiceSingapore digital payment token service activity.
Hong Kong MSOMoney Service Operator license for money changing and remittance.
Hong Kong SVFStored Value Facility license for wallet and stored-value operations.
Japan Crypto-Asset Exchange RegistrationJFSA registration for crypto-asset exchange services.
Dubai VARA VASP LicenseDubai virtual asset licensing regime for specific virtual asset activities.

Stablecoin Licenses

Stablecoin activity can touch e-money, payment services, virtual asset services, custody, brokerage, exchange, reserve management, and consumer protection rules.

Stablecoin issuer model

IssuerThe entity minting, redeeming, and operating the stablecoin.
Reserve AssetCash, bank deposits, government bills, or other assets backing the token.
Segregated ReserveReserve assets separated from the issuer's operating assets.
AttestationA periodic third-party report on reserves and liabilities.
Redemption RightThe holder's right to redeem tokens for fiat or equivalent value.
Run RiskThe risk that many holders redeem at once and reserves or liquidity are insufficient.

Regulatory categories

MiCA EMTEU e-money token category under the Markets in Crypto-Assets regulation.
MiCA ARTEU asset-referenced token category for tokens referencing multiple assets or baskets.
CASPCrypto-Asset Service Provider under MiCA.
Payment StablecoinA stablecoin intended for payment or settlement use rather than only trading.
Custody LicenseAuthorization to hold crypto assets or private keys for clients.
Exchange / Broker LicenseAuthorization for buying, selling, or arranging trades in crypto assets.

Operational obligations

Reserve ManagementInvestment, liquidity, and safeguarding controls for backing assets.
Whitepaper / DisclosurePublic disclosures describing rights, risks, reserves, technology, and issuer obligations.
Redemption SLAThe promised timeframe for redeeming stablecoins into fiat.
Chain GovernanceRules for minting, freezing, burning, blacklist controls, and supported networks.
Financial Crime ControlsKYC, wallet screening, sanctions, Travel Rule, and transaction monitoring for stablecoin flows.
Consumer Complaint ProcessRequired process for handling user complaints, errors, and disputes.

Institutional Roles

Modern payment products are assembled from many institutional roles: licensed entities, bank partners, processors, networks, liquidity providers, compliance vendors, custodians, and program managers.

Merchant-facing roles

Merchant of Record / MoRThe legal seller responsible for taxes, refunds, disputes, and customer payment obligations.
Seller of RecordThe party recorded as the seller in commerce and tax workflows.
PlatformA marketplace, SaaS product, or network coordinating merchants, buyers, and payments.
Sub-merchantA merchant onboarded under a PayFac or platform structure.
BeneficiaryThe recipient of funds.
CounterpartyThe other side of a transaction or settlement obligation.

Infrastructure roles

Banking PartnerA bank providing accounts, safeguarding, payment rails, sponsorship, or settlement services.
ProcessorThe transaction-processing infrastructure layer for authorization, clearing, settlement, or reporting.
NetworkA card, bank, or blockchain network setting rules and routing transactions.
CustodianA licensed entity holding client assets or private keys.
Liquidity ProviderA partner supplying fiat, FX, crypto, or stablecoin liquidity.
Compliance VendorA provider for KYC, KYB, AML, sanctions, fraud, device, or blockchain analytics.

Risk ownership

PrincipalThe licensed entity ultimately responsible for a regulated activity.
AgentAn entity acting on behalf of a principal under permission or registration.
OutsourcingDelegating operations to a vendor while retaining regulatory responsibility.
Material OutsourcingA critical outsourced function that needs governance, monitoring, and sometimes regulatory notification.

Transaction Lifecycle

The complete journey of a transaction before it is authorized, captured, cleared, settled, reconciled, refunded, disputed, paid out, and closed in the ledger.

Card lifecycle

AuthorizationThe issuer approves a payment request and places a hold on funds.
Partial AuthorizationThe issuer approves only part of the requested amount.
Incremental AuthorizationAn additional authorization used when the final amount increases, common in hotels or car rentals.
CaptureThe merchant confirms the transaction should be charged.
ClearingTransaction data and fees are exchanged between institutions.
SettlementNet funds move between banks or counterparties.

After settlement

PayoutThe acquirer or PSP sends funds to the merchant bank account or wallet.
RefundThe merchant sends money back after a transaction has settled.
ChargebackA cardholder dispute routed through the issuer and card network.
ReversalA transaction adjustment that cancels or corrects a previous message.
AdjustmentA settlement correction for fees, disputes, errors, or manual operations.
Write-offRecognizing that a receivable is unlikely to be collected.

Stablecoin lifecycle

Payment RequestThe invoice, checkout session, QR code, or address generated for the payer.
BroadcastThe payer signs and submits the transaction to the network.
MempoolThe set of pending transactions waiting for block inclusion.
ConfirmationA block includes the transaction; additional blocks increase confidence.
FinalityThe point where the payment is practically irreversible.
Webhook ConfirmationThe merchant system receives a server notification and updates order status.

Business Metrics

The metrics payment teams, operators, and investors use to understand scale, margin, retention, risk, conversion, and capital efficiency.

Volume and monetization

TPV · Total Payment VolumeThe value of payments processed through the platform.
GMV · Gross Merchandise VolumeTotal commerce value, including transactions not processed by the platform's own payment stack.
Take RateNet revenue divided by TPV or GMV, depending on the business model.
Net RevenueRevenue after pass-through costs such as interchange, scheme fees, network fees, or partner costs.
Gross MarginRevenue minus direct costs, expressed as a percentage of revenue.
Contribution MarginMargin after variable costs such as processing, risk losses, support, and incentives.

Retention and customer economics

NRR · Net Revenue RetentionExisting-customer revenue after expansion, contraction, and churn.
GRR · Gross Revenue RetentionRevenue retained before expansion.
LTV · Lifetime ValueTotal expected contribution from a customer over its lifecycle.
CAC · Customer Acquisition CostSales and marketing cost required to acquire a customer.
Payback PeriodTime needed to recover CAC from gross profit.
ChurnCustomer, account, or revenue loss over a period.

Payment operations metrics

Authorization RateApproved authorization attempts divided by total attempts.
Conversion RateCheckout sessions that successfully become paid transactions.
Dispute RateDisputed transactions divided by successful transactions or volume.
Fraud Loss RateFraud losses as a percentage of payment volume.
Settlement SLAExpected time from transaction to merchant payout or wallet receipt.
Reconciliation Break RateShare of transactions that fail automatic matching and need manual investigation.

Recurring revenue metrics

MRRMonthly recurring revenue from subscriptions or platform fees.
ARRAnnual recurring revenue, commonly MRR multiplied by twelve.
ARPUAverage revenue per user or account.
PEPMPer employee per month, common in HR, benefits, payroll, and corporate card pricing.
